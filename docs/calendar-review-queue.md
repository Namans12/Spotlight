# Calendar reconciliation review queue

Run `npm run calendar:review-queue -- --days 90` with `DATABASE_URL` set. The command reads a bounded upcoming date window in a read-only transaction and prints JSON, including the scanned count, reason counts, source links and actionable rows. It makes no network requests to sources and never updates records. `--as-of YYYY-MM-DD`, `--days 1..365` and `--limit 1..5000` support repeatable, bounded reviews. The `truncated` flag means the earliest rows filled the limit; increase it or narrow the date window before treating counts as complete.

The queue prioritizes date and identity conflicts, then unreviewed entries, then other uncertainties. Reason counts overlap. `duplicate_observation` identifies Wikipedia records with identical stored content apart from ID, creation time, source key and the legacy `is_theatrical` flag in the scanned window, with the other record IDs in `duplicateObservationIds`; it is not a deletion instruction, and groups may be incomplete if the result is truncated. The flag is ignored because older Wikipedia imports set it without evidence, while newer imports do not. Source, date, title, evidence and other stored fields still have to match. Other matching titles or dates alone do not prove duplication. `global_premiere_local_unknown` distinguishes a source-matched global first-air observation from `region_unknown`; neither proves an India premiere. Missing identity and uncertain region remain review items even when a date is corroborated elsewhere. The queue does not assert that a source is reachable or still supports its old claim.

`identity_conflict` also flags an explicitly reviewed mismatch between an editorial title and its stored TMDB identity. This differs from an identity that has merely not yet been verified; suppression of a link alone does not prove a mismatch. Reviews remain bound to their exact source record and require rechecking when stale.

Freshness uses the source observation time (or a curated review's checked date), with 30 days as the review threshold. Invalid, future or absent timestamps have unknown freshness. A fresh observation is not a guarantee of correctness. Reviewed dates can remain disputed even while fresh. Recheck source identity, event type, territory, date and terms before adding or changing an evidence decision; do not change production data based solely on a queue item. Existing manual annotations are matched to their original record identity and must be reviewed if that identity changes.

An independent conflict warning remains attached if the same TMDB-origin event later receives compatible TMDB date evidence. A refresh alone does not settle competing sources; mismatched identities or territories do not inherit the warning.

TMDB date evidence without a linked title ID, movie/TV media type, or a TMDB source URL is marked `evidence_incomplete`. A region in the queue is the stored or reviewed claim, not by itself proof that the evidence is complete or that the row is eligible for public display.

Automated evidence rechecks only write evidence for records originating from the exact TMDB title URL. A matching name and date alone cannot establish that an editorial record refers to the same title. Older editorial records may already have TMDB date evidence from earlier rechecks; the queue flags these as `identity_unverified`, and the calendar withholds the title link, poster and claimed region until identity is independently reviewed. Stored records are not modified.

Identity reviews in `lib/calendarIdentity.ts` match the original source, title, language, event date, TMDB ID and regional evidence, and cite the independent cast or crew comparison. Their review freshness is tracked separately from the TMDB date observation: `identity_review_stale` means the identity review needs a recheck and does not refresh the original release evidence.

The queue checks observation and manual review timestamps independently. `evidence_stale` and `freshness_unknown` refer to the stored observation; `review_stale` and `review_freshness_unknown` refer to a separate manual review. The `evidenceObservedAt` and `reviewCheckedAt` fields expose those timestamps; the legacy `checkedAt` field is retained for compatibility. A fresh review does not refresh an old observation, and a refreshed observation does not refresh an old review.

Some reviews establish only that two sources describe the same title. For these, the title link can be restored while the editorial event's region remains unknown; a US schedule and an India TMDB date do not become one event just because they share a day.

Some manually reviewed regional announcements can be scoped to a country in the calendar response without modifying the original record. For example, the Telugu *Talcum Powder* entry is matched against its exact source, date, language and unresolved identity, and is shown only for India based on its linked BookMyShow listing. The listing is an announced date, not a confirmed showtime or an identified TMDB title. The queue retains the missing-identity and duplicate-observation reasons; the review's age is displayed and must be rechecked when stale.
